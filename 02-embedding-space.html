Part I

Why Vectors? From Name Tags to Coordinates

The embedding layer is the moment the model stops memorizing labels and starts measuring meaning.

SECTION 2.1

The 50,000-Dimensional Dead End

The obvious way to feed token 4051 to a neural network: a vector of 50,000 zeros with a single 1 in slot 4051. It is mathematically clean — and completely useless. Here is the three-part autopsy.

🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to name three fatal flaws of one-hot encoding — scale, sparsity, and semantic blindness — and compute exactly why every pair of one-hot vectors is the same distance apart.

Flaw 1 · The Scale Trap

GPT-2's vocabulary is 50,257 tokens. A one-hot vector per token is 50,257 numbers — of which 50,256 are zeros. A linear layer mapping that to a 1,600-dim hidden state needs 50,257 × 1,600 ≈ 80.4 million weights just to look words up.

80.4Mweights, just to read input — before any thinking happens

Flaw 2 · The Sparsity Desert

99.998% of every one-hot vector is zero. Almost every input activates almost no weights. You are paying memory and compute for a vector that carries one bit of information — which slot is on — stretched across fifty thousand dimensions.

0.002%of the vector does any work

Flaw 3 · Semantic Blindness

Worst of all: every pair of one-hot vectors is identical geometrically. "cat" and "dog" are exactly as far apart as "cat" and "refrigerator" — dot product 0, distance √2, always. The space contains no meaning at all.

√2distance between ANY two different tokens, forever

LAB 2.1 The Geometry Void — one-hot vs embedded
Six words in one-hot space: every word sits on its own axis, every pair exactly √2 apart, dot product always 0. The space has axes but no geography. Flip to the embedding view to see what meaning looks like.
🔮 Predict Before You Compute
In a 50,257-token one-hot space, how many distinct values does the pairwise distance between two different tokens take?
Exactly 1. Every one-hot vector has norm 1 and every pair of distinct ones is orthogonal: dot product 0, so distance = √(1² + 1²) = √2. There is no "closer" or "farther" — the metric structure is perfectly flat. This is the void an embedding space must fill: distances that vary in proportion to meaning.
SECTION 2.2

The fix is almost disappointingly simple: keep a giant matrix where row i is the learned vector for token i. Token 4051 arrives → pull row 4051. That pull is the embedding layer. Everything else is training.

📓 Instruction Manual · The Lookup Is a Matmul (That Nobody Runs)

Page 1 · The Official Story

∙Open any deep-learning textbook: "the embedding layer multiplies the one-hot vector x by the embedding matrix E." Formally, for token index i:

$$x = \text{onehot}(i) \in \{0,1\}^{V}, \qquad h = x\,E \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$$

Since x is all zeros except a 1 at position i, the product x·E picks out exactly row i of E and adds nothing else. Try it on paper with a 4×3 matrix — the zeros annihilate every row but one.

Page 2 · The Engineering Reality

Multiplying a 50,000-vector of almost-all-zeros by a 50,000×768 matrix wastes 50,000 × 768 multiply-accumulates to fetch 768 numbers. So every real framework — PyTorch's nn.Embedding, JAX's take, TensorFlow's gather — skips the multiply and indexes the row directly.

Same math, 50,000× less work. "One-hot times E" is the specification; "gather row i" is the implementation. You will meet this spec-vs-implementation split again with FlashAttention in Module 11.

Page 3 · What the Numbers Mean

Nobody assigned "royalty" to dimension 0 of our toy space — and no one assigns meanings inside GPT-4 either. Dimensions become meaningful as a side effect of training: gradient descent stretches and rotates the space until useful distinctions separate. Some axes end up tracking tense, plurality, formality, sentiment — most track nothing a human would name.

Our 8 dimensions are hand-labeled so you can see the geometry. Real spaces (GPT-2: 768 dims, GPT-3: 12,288, Llama-3: 4,096) work identically — you just can't read the axis labels.

🔮 Predict Before You Compute
During backpropagation, which rows of E receive gradient updates for a training example containing the tokens [4, 4, 19]?
Only rows 4 and 19. The lookup is a gather: rows that were never touched contributed nothing to the forward pass, so the chain rule sends them zero gradient. This is why rare words have poorly-trained embeddings — GPT-3's infamous glitch tokens (like petertodd) were seen so rarely in training that their rows stayed near random init, and the model visibly malfunctioned around them.
SECTION 2.3

"You Shall Know a Word by the Company It Keeps"

Firth, 1957. The distributional hypothesis: words that appear in similar contexts mean similar things. word2vec (Mikolov et al., Google 2013) turned that slogan into an algorithm — and the embedding era began.

🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to explain the skip-gram training task, why negative sampling makes it 1000× cheaper, and why geometry emerges from prediction without anyone programming it.

The Skip-Gram Trick

Build a deliberately tiny network: given a center word, predict its neighbors. One hidden layer, no nonlinearity. The catch — we never use the network for prediction. After training, we throw away the output layer and steal the weight matrix. Those weights are the embeddings.

$$P(w_{ctx} \mid w_{c}) = \frac{\exp(v'_{w_{ctx}} \cdot v_{w_c})}{\sum_{k=1}^{V} \exp(v'_{w_k} \cdot v_{w_c})}$$

The genius shortcut is negative sampling: instead of normalizing over all 50,000 words (a 50,000-way softmax per step), compare the true neighbor against ~5 randomly drawn "negative" words. Same geometry, 1000× less compute.

💡
Why geometry emerges: two words that predict similar neighbors must hold similar vectors — because the same output weights read both. Nobody programmed "cats ≈ dogs." The prediction task forced it. Meaning is a side effect of compression.
📜
Historical note: word2vec trained on 100 billion words in a day on 2013 hardware. The "king − man + woman ≈ queen" demo made global headlines. GloVe (Stanford, 2014) followed with the same idea from co-occurrence counts instead of prediction.

🔮 Predict Before You Compute
Two words — "hot" and "warm" — appear in nearly identical contexts ("a ___ day", "keep it ___"). After skip-gram training, what happens to their embedding vectors?
They converge. Both words must predict the same neighbors through the same output weights, and the cheapest way to do that is to hold nearly the same input vector. Synonymy becomes proximity — automatically. This is the entire secret of embedding spaces, hiding in one gradient update.
📓 Instruction Manual · Three Eras of Embeddings

Era 1 · word2vec (2013)

2013Mikolov et al., Google. The skip-gram trick you just drove: predict neighbors, steal the weights. Two published variants — Skip-gram (predict context from center) and CBOW (predict center from context) — plus negative sampling to make the softmax cheap. Trained on 100 billion words in about a day on 2013 hardware. The demo that broke the internet: king − man + woman ≈ queen.

word2vec was the moment "meaning" became arithmetic. It kicked off a gold rush: every NLP task in 2014-2017 started by averaging word2vec vectors and hoping.

Era 2 · GloVe (2014)

2014Pennington et al., Stanford. The question: do you need a prediction network at all? GloVe says no — factor a co-occurrence matrix directly. Count how often words appear together across a corpus, weight rare pairs down, and least-squares fit the log-co-occurrence. Same geometry, no network, no sampling tricks.

The lesson: embeddings are a statistical object, not a neural-network artifact. Word counts contain the geometry; the network is just one extraction tool.

Era 3 · ELMo → BERT (2018)

2018Static vectors cap out at one-meaning-per-word. ELMo (Peters et al.) trained a bidirectional LSTM and produced a different vector per sentence. BERT (Devlin et al.) did it with transformers, and contextual embeddings — Section 2.11's whole topic — became the standard. The lookup table never died; it just became the first layer of a journey instead of the destination.

Module 02 ends where modern LLMs begin: the embedding row is the starting point, and attention (Module 03) rewrites it sentence by sentence.

The measurement

Select word A and word B on the map or from the chips above. The Observatory computes cosine similarity and Euclidean distance live, and draws the angle θ between the two vectors from the origin.

cosine_similarity(a, b) = (a · b) / (‖a‖ ‖b‖) — the cosine of the angle θ. 1.0 means same direction, 0 means unrelated, −1 means opposite.
Default tour: king ↔ queen measured at load. Try cat ↔ dog (close), then cat ↔ paris (far), then king ↔ prince (very close). Notice: clusters in this map are real structure from the 8-D space — PCA only flattens it, it never invents it.
$$\cos\theta = \frac{a \cdot b}{\|a\|\,\|b\|} = \frac{\sum_i a_i b_i}{\sqrt{\sum_i a_i^2}\,\sqrt{\sum_i b_i^2}} \in [-1, 1]$$

Why cosine won: it divides out length, so a "loud" vector and a "quiet" one pointing the same way score 1.0. Word frequency inflates magnitudes; direction carries the meaning. Cosine listens to direction only.

$$d(a,b) = \sqrt{\sum_i (a_i - b_i)^2} \in [0, \infty)$$

Euclidean distance is the ruler: honest straight-line distance, but magnitude-sensitive. Two vectors can point identically (cosine 1.0) yet measure far apart if one is longer. Section 2.6 makes them fight — with sliders.

Subtracting B from A keeps only what distinguishes A from B (here: the ROYAL axis). Adding C applies that distinction to a new base. The nearest real word to the result is the answer.

⚠️
When the machine fails: analogies break when a relationship isn't a consistent direction in the space. "doctor − man + woman" famously landed near "nurse" in word2vec — the space had absorbed 2013's occupational gender bias from the training text. The geometry is honest: it faithfully encodes the statistics of its corpus, including the ugly ones. Debiasing embeddings is its own research subfield (Bolukbasi et al., 2016).

🔮 Predict Before You Compute
Vector a and vector b point in exactly the same direction, but ‖b‖ = 3‖a‖. What are cosine similarity and Euclidean distance?
cosine = 1.0, distance = 2‖a‖. Cosine divides by both norms, so the factor of 3 cancels: (a·3a)/(‖a‖·3‖a‖) = 1. Euclidean measures the tip-to-tip gap: |3‖a‖ − ‖a‖| = 2‖a‖ along the same ray. Same direction, different point — the two metrics are answering two different questions, and only one of them is "do these words mean the same thing?"
Part III

Position — The Order Problem

A bag of vectors has no "first" and "next". Teaching geometry to respect order takes waves, rotations, or a simple bribe.

SECTION 2.7

Attention Is Colorblind to Order

Self-attention compares every token to every token as a set. Shuffle the input, and every pairwise comparison is identical — the model literally cannot tell "dog bites man" from "man bites dog". Position must be injected. That injection is this entire Part.

🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to demonstrate order-invariance from the attention formula itself, and explain the three families of fixes: additive waves, rotated pairs, and distance penalties.
LAB 2.7 The Shuffle Machine — scramble the sentence, nothing changes
Without positional encoding, attention computes the same score matrix for any ordering — here the shuffled sentence produces the identical set of dot products, just relabeled. Order information: zero. The fix arrives in the next three sections.
🧮
From the formula: attention computes softmax(QKᵀ/√d)V. Permute the rows of Q and K by the same permutation P and you get P·softmax(QKᵀ/√d)V — the same matrix, row-shuffled. No term in the formula knows which token came first. Mathematicians call this permutation equivariance; engineers call it "we need positional encoding."
SECTION 2.8

The Wave Factory — Sinusoidal Encoding (2017)

The original Attention Is All You Need solution: add a unique "barcode of waves" to each position's embedding. Each dimension oscillates at its own frequency, so every position gets a fingerprint no other position shares.

🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to compute PE values by hand for any (pos, dimension) pair, explain why low dimensions wiggle fast and high dimensions wiggle slowly, and state why this scheme extrapolates to unseen lengths.

🧮 Worked Example — PE(2, ·) by hand, d = 8

Pair 0 (dims 0,1): angle = pos / 100000/8 = 2 / 1 = 2.000 rad

PE(2,0) = sin(2.000) = 0.909 · PE(2,1) = cos(2.000) = −0.416

Pair 1 (dims 2,3): angle = 2 / 100002/8 = 2 / 10 = 0.200 rad

PE(2,2) = sin(0.200) = 0.199 · PE(2,3) = cos(0.200) = 0.980

Pair 2 (dims 4,5): angle = 2 / 100004/8 = 2 / 100 = 0.020 rad

PE(2,4) = sin(0.020) ≈ 0.020 · PE(2,5) = cos(0.020) ≈ 1.000

Pair 3 (dims 6,7): angle = 2 / 100006/8 = 2 / 1000 = 0.002 rad

PE(2,6) ≈ 0.002 · PE(2,7) ≈ 1.000

→ Position 2's barcode: [0.909, −0.416, 0.199, 0.980, 0.020, 1.000, 0.002, 1.000] — unique across all positions.

🔮 Predict Before You Compute
A model trained with sinusoidal encoding on sequences of length 512 is given a sequence of length 2,048. What happens to its positional barcodes?
They keep working. The formula is a closed form — position 2,048 gets a perfectly valid, unique barcode computed the same way as position 2. This extrapolation property is exactly what learned absolute embeddings (GPT-2's wpe matrix) lack: a learned row for position 2,048 simply doesn't exist. (In practice, attention still degrades on far-longer sequences — but the encoding itself isn't what breaks.)
SECTION 2.9 ★ SIGNATURE

The RoPE Observatory — Position as Rotation

Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE, Su et al. 2021) encodes position by rotating each pair of query/key dimensions by an angle proportional to position. Llama, PaLM, GPT-NeoX, Qwen — nearly every modern model uses it. This Observatory shows you why, with two hands on a dial.

🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to prove (by watching) that RoPE's attention score depends only on relative distance m − n, and explain why that is exactly what language needs.

Each hand starts pointing at angle 0° (the un-rotated vector). Position m rotates Q by m·θ; position n rotates K by n·θ. The score q·k = cos((m−n)·θ) hears only the difference. Lock the distance and slide both positions together — the score does not move.
The invariant: attention between "the" at 3 and "cat" at 1 equals attention between "the" at 23 and "cat" at 21 — same gap, same score. Language works this way too: "the cat" means the same thing at the start of a paragraph as at the end. Note: the dial exaggerates θ to 15°/position so the rotation is legible — the real θ₀ = 1 rad ≈ 57°, and deeper pairs rotate slower, giving RoPE a full spectrum of distance sensitivities.
📓 Instruction Manual · Why Rotation Gives You Relativity For Free

Page 1 · The 2-D Rotation

Group the d dimensions into pairs. For pair i with frequency θ_i = 10000^{−2i/d}, RoPE rotates that pair's 2-D plane by the angle m·θ_i when the token sits at position m:

$$R_{\Theta,m}^{(i)} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos m\theta_i & -\sin m\theta_i \\ \sin m\theta_i & \cos m\theta_i \end{pmatrix}, \qquad q' = R_{\Theta,m}\, q, \quad k' = R_{\Theta,n}\, k$$

Zero learned parameters — the rotation angles are fixed by position and dimension, exactly like the sinusoidal waves. But unlike waves, rotation acts on Q and K inside attention, not on the embedding.

Page 2 · The Relativity Proof (one line)

Rotations compose by adding angles, and every rotation matrix satisfies R_m^T R_n = R_{n-m}. So the attention score becomes:

$$(R_m q)^{\top}(R_n k) = q^{\top} R_m^{\top} R_n\, k = q^{\top} R_{\,n-m}\, k$$

The absolute positions m and n cancel out of the score — only the gap n − m survives. The model literally cannot hear where in the sequence the pair sits; it can only hear how far apart the two tokens are. That is the entire theorem, and the dial in the Observatory is this equation drawn.

Page 3 · Why a Spectrum of Frequencies

Pair 0 rotates fast (θ₀ = 1 rad/position: full circle every ~6 tokens) — sensitive to fine, local distances. The last pair barely rotates (θ = 1/10000: circle every ~63,000 tokens) — sensitive to long-range structure. RoPE stacks dozens of these gears, so attention can measure "2 tokens apart" and "2,000 tokens apart" with the same mechanism.

Llama-3 tunes the base wavelength (θ_base = 500,000 instead of 10,000) so its slowest gear spans its full 128K context. One constant, retuned, buys 16× longer memory — you'll meet this again as "RoPE scaling" in Module 13.

🔮 Predict Before You Compute
GPT-2 (learned absolute, trained at 1,024 context) is handed a 2,000-token document. What is the exact failure?
The rows don't exist. A learned wpe matrix has exactly 1,024 rows; asking for row 1,500 is an index error, not a degradation. This is the hard wall that closed-form schemes (sinusoidal, RoPE, ALiBi) never hit — and why learned absolute encoding, despite powering GPT-2 and GPT-3, was abandoned by the Llama generation. SECTION 2.13 · CAPSTONE
🎯 This is a retrieval-practice exercise: answer from memory, not by scrolling up. Each scenario is a real failure mode from real systems. 4/5 earns the "The Word Cartographer" collectible brick.
