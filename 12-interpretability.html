Part I

The Question

The model works. Why should we care whether we understand it? And what would understanding even look like?

SECTION 12.1

Why Interpret — Trust, Debugging, Safety, Science

Every benchmark says the model is smart. Interpretability asks a different question: is it smart for the reasons we think? A model can pass an eval by memorizing patterns, by gaming the test, or by reasoning — and all three look identical from the outside.

🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to name the four reasons interpretability matters, and state the one honest fact about weight tables that makes "just read the weights" a dead end.

Trust

A model that is right for the wrong reasons is fragile. It found a shortcut — "the word not appears, so answer negatively" — and the shortcut breaks on the first distribution shift. Interpretability is how you check that the answer came from the reasoning you paid for, not a spurious pattern.

shortcut ≠ skillthe core failure mode trust asks about

Debugging

Models fail in weird ways: they answer the same prompt differently with one token changed, or follow instructions they were never trained on. You cannot file a bug report against a lookup table. Interpretability gives you a place to look — a layer, a head, a feature — instead of a black box to poke.

a place to lookwhat a debugging story needs and a black box refuses

Safety

A backdoored model passes every eval and misbehaves only when a trigger appears — the trigger is invisible in outputs. Activations are where the trigger lives. Auditing what a model is actually computing, not just what it outputs, is the difference between shipping a product and shipping a liability.

outputs lieactivations are the audit trail

Science

How do transformers actually do in-context learning? Where do features live? What is the residual stream for? These are real open questions — and each answered one (the induction head, the feature dictionary, the logit lens) became a tool every lab now uses. This module is the closest thing the course has to a research bench.

Unlabeled, not encrypted. There is no codebook: a weight of 0.31 in row 67, column 9 is meaningful only in combination with the 23,231 other numbers and the operations that consume them. SECTION 12.2 ★ THE FIRST WINDOW

The Logit Lens — Reading the Residual Stream Layer by Layer

The residual stream is shared: every layer reads it and writes back to it, and the final unembedding reads only the last write. So nothing stops you from unembedding any intermediate write — and reading what the model "believes" at that moment. One formula, zero training:

$$\text{prediction}_\ell = \text{softmax}\big(\text{RMSNorm}(h_\ell)\, W_{U}\big) \qquad h_\ell = \text{the residual stream after layer } \ell$$
🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to run a logit lens yourself, read a layer-by-layer trace, and state the two things the lens can never show you.

Why it works — the stream as a shared notebook

Every layer writes its output into the residual stream, and every later layer reads the whole accumulated notebook. The final prediction is a read of the final page. The lens simply peeks at earlier pages: the "belief" of the model after layer 1, 2, and 3, before the final readout. On a trained model you watch the top-1 token converge from generic to specific — the model decides early and refines late.

On this module's honest engine, the weights were never trained, so the lens shows you something subtler and more important: the stream is really being rewritten between layers (the top-1 token genuinely changes), and the last layer's write is what the readout reads. The mechanism is visible even though no knowledge is in it. That is the lens working.

🔭
The origin story: nostalgebraist's 2020 blog post "interpreting GPT: the logit lens" pointed the unembedding at every layer of GPT-2 and found the model "thinks" the answer long before it outputs it — layers 4-5 often already know the final token. Belrose et al. (2023) then showed a trained per-layer affine map (the tuned lens) reads even earlier layers more cleanly — with the honest caveat that a tuned lens can "skip ahead" to the output and hide non-verbal computation (the 2026 J-lens critique from Anthropic).

The honest limits — what the lens does NOT show

No mechanism

The lens shows what the stream looks like. It cannot show which head wrote it, which input it came from, or why. Two models with identical lens traces could compute the same answer through completely different machinery.

Linear-only vision

The lens reads directions a single matrix can see. Computation that lives in non-linear combinations, or that is only meaningful after more layers, is invisible to it — which is exactly what the tuned lens and J-lens critiques are about.

No causality

"Layer 2 already predicts the answer" does not mean layer 2 caused the answer. The stream could be copying the answer upward while the real computation happens elsewhere. Correlation is on the left side of this page; the knife for causality is section 12.3.

Field Notes — the lens, from blog post to frontier
2020

The blog post that started a field

nostalgebraist points GPT-2's unembedding matrix at every layer's residual stream and publishes the result as "the logit lens." The finding: the model's final answer is often already the top prediction four layers before the end. People had assumed the early layers were "feature extraction" — the lens showed they are already predicting. One line of code, one formula, and the residual stream became readable.

The lens is a probe, not a window: it reads what a single matrix can read. What it cannot read, it cannot show — and that silence is information too.
2023

The tuned lens — a better projector

Belrose et al. (NeurIPS 2023) train a small affine map per layer to project residuals into vocabulary space instead of reusing the final unembedding. Early layers become much more readable. But the paper's own caveat is now famous: a tuned lens can "skip ahead" — it learns to approximate the whole downstream stack, so a clean early-layer readout may describe the output, not the layer. Readability is not understanding.

2026

The J-lens — what the model does not say

Anthropic's 2026 work on "verbalizable representations" builds the Jacobian lens: a learned map that simulates all downstream computation per layer. The striking finding: only ~6-7% of activation variance lies in J-space — yet it is the part that drives verbal report. The rest is computation that influences behavior without ever becoming sayable. The lens family's last lesson: a model can do more than it can say, and no lens shows both.

Correlation, not causation. Part II

The Knife — Cutting Causation from Correlation

The lens shows what the stream looks like. Patching shows what the stream does.

SECTION 12.3 ★ SIGNATURE

Activation Patching — The Causal Knife

The strongest tool in the interpretability kit: run the model twice, swap one activation between the runs, and watch the output move. If swapping this cell moves that output, the cell caused it — no stories, no guessing.

🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to run a patching experiment, read a layer × position effect grid, and explain why patching proves causation where the lens only shows correlation.
🧮 Worked Example — One patch, by the numbers

Setup: run A on "the cat sat", run B on "the dog sat". Both predict the next token after position 10.

The patch: take B's run and overwrite layer 2's residual vector at position 10 with A's vector from the same cell.

B'[L2, pos10] ← A[L2, pos10]

Measure: how far does B's output distribution move toward A's? KL divergence between the patched and unpatched distributions.

KL(B'_out ‖ B_out) — computed live in the lab below

Read the grid: the lab patches every layer × position cell. The hot cells (high KL) are where A and B actually differ causally; cold cells are where the runs agree or the computation is elsewhere.

→ On the default pair, the last-position cells light up: that's where the next-token readout happens. Content cells (where "cat" vs "dog" live) are hot too — the difference enters there.

LAB 12.3 The patch grid — every layer × position, one swap at a time
Default pair: A = "the cat sat" · B = "the dog sat". Each cell = KL(output_B_patched ‖ output_B). Bright = that cell causally matters for the next-token readout. Click 🔍 then a cell to see the exact numbers.
🔪
Why this is the gold standard: the lens can only say "this layer looks like it has the answer" — correlation. Patching says "if I remove this, the answer moves" — causation. Every serious claim in mechanistic interpretability — circuits, features, heads — is verified by patching (or its cousin, activation ablation).
📓 Instruction Manual · The Counterfactual Logic of Patching

Page 1 · The Counterfactual Question

"What if this activation had been the other one?" That is a counterfactual. The model is a deterministic function — we can actually run it with the swapped value. No sampling, no approximation: the patched run is the exact answer to the counterfactual.

$$\text{Effect}(l,i) = \text{KL}\big(\,p_{out}(x \mid \text{patch}(l,i)) \,\|\, p_{out}(x) \,\big)$$

Page 2 · Why Correlation Isn't Enough

The residual stream is a shared notebook: layer 2 might contain the answer because layer 1 wrote it and layer 2 just passes it along. The lens can't tell writer from courier. Patching layer 2 would show a small effect (the courier's value doesn't matter — the writer's does).

The lens tells you where the answer is visible. Patching tells you where it is computed. They agree at the writer and disagree at the courier — that disagreement is the whole point of this section.

Page 3 · The Confound That Remains

Patching is not magic. It tests one counterfactual at a time; circuits with parallel pathways need joint patches; and a patched run can land in out-of-distribution territory the model never visits. Every tool has a blind spot — the honest researcher names it.

Page 1 of 3
🔮 Predict Before You Compute
You patch B's layer-0 content cell (where "dog" sits) with A's "cat" activation. What should happen to B's next-token prediction?
It shifts toward A's prediction. The content difference between the two inputs enters at layer 0; replacing it replaces the content. Run the grid and hover the L0 content cells — their KL is nonzero precisely because this swap moves the output. That movement is the causal proof.

The Attention Read — What Each Head Is Doing

The engine has 9 attention heads (3 layers × 3). Each one is a small routing machine. Read them: for every position, where does the head actually attend? Which positions does it copy from?

🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to characterize a head's behavior from its attention pattern and state honestly when a head has no obvious job.
LAB 12.4 Head inspector — pick a head, read its attention
Default: attention weights per position on "the cat sat". Bars = where the selected head listens. A "copy" head would pour weight on position i−1; a "first-token" head on position 0. Many heads on untrained weights have no clear job — that is an honest reading, not a bug.
🎯
The real-world goal: on trained models, heads form recognizable roles — induction heads that copy the token after the previous occurrence (Olsson et al. 2022), prefix-matching heads, position heads. Researchers catalog these across thousands of heads (the "head zoo"). On our untrained engine, you will mostly find noise — the honest version of the same exercise.
🔮 Predict Before You Inspect
How many induction heads (heads that copy "the token after the previous match") should we find on this untrained engine?
Zero. Induction requires the model to have learned a copying pattern over its training data — it is a learned circuit, not a mechanical freebie. Finding none here is the honest result; finding them in a real model (Llama-2, GPT-2) is evidence of a genuinely discovered circuit.
Part III

The Dictionary — Decomposing Thought into Features

A dense vector is a blur of everything. Sparse features are sharp: "space", "quotation", "math mode" — each a clean direction.

SECTION 12.5

Sparse Autoencoders — Building the Feature Dictionary

A residual vector is a superposition of many things at once. An SAE learns a dictionary of directions — "features" — and writes each activation as a sparse combination of a few of them. Sparse = readable.

🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to decompose an activation into features, explain why sparsity is the key constraint, and read a reconstruction honestly — including where it fails.
🧮 Worked Example — The Dictionary Trade

The encoder: given activation x and dictionary F (6 feature vectors, normalized), score each feature by alignment, keep the positive part.

f(x) = ReLU(Fᵀ x) — coefficients, one per feature

The decoder: rebuild the activation from the chosen features.

x̂ = F · f(x) — reconstruction

The honesty check: compare the rebuild to the original.

error = ‖x − x̂‖ / ‖x‖ — computed live below

Why sparsity: real SAEs train the dictionary so that each activation uses only ~0.1% of features. Our toy dictionary is hand-picked (6 vocab directions) and the coefficients are thresholded dot products — a toy SAE, honest label. The error you see is real, and it is the price of a tiny dictionary.

→ Real SAEs (Anthropic 2023-2026) train millions of features on frontier models; the math is this same encoder/decoder loop at scale.

📓 Instruction Manual · The SAE Lineage — from monosemanticity to the frontier

Page 1 · The Math (real SAEs)

Train a dictionary of n_features directions so that every activation reconstructs with few active features:

$$f(x) = \mathrm{ReLU}(W_e x + b_e), \qquad \hat{x} = W_d f(x) + b_d, \qquad \mathcal{L} = \|x - \hat{x}\|^2 + \lambda \|f(x)\|_1$$

The ℓ₁ penalty buys sparsity; the reconstruction term buys fidelity. The two fight — that fight is the entire SAE research program.

Page 2 · The Timeline

2023Anthropic's "Towards Monosemanticity": SAEs on small models found interpretable features — the Golden Gate Bridge neuron, the "DNA" feature, the "recursion" feature. Each feature is a direction the model actually uses.

2024Scaling: SAEs on frontier models (millions of features), open-source tooling (Neuronpedia, SAELens).

2025Feature steering: nudge a feature direction, change the model's behavior — interpretability becomes control.

Page 3 · The Honest Limits

Features are correlational: a "space" feature firing on spaces doesn't prove the model "thinks in spaces" — it proves a direction is available. Whether the model uses it causally is a patching question (Section 12.3). The best papers do both: find features, then patch them.

Your toy SAE above demonstrates the method with honest error. Real SAEs are the same loop with 1,000,000× more features and a trained dictionary.

🔮 Predict Before You Compute
With only 6 hand-picked feature directions, what should the reconstruction error look like?
Clearly nonzero, but not total. Six vocabulary directions capture the components of the activation that "look like" those characters — often 10-20% of the norm on our toy engine — and lose the rest. That middle ground is exactly why real SAEs need millions of features: every lost percent is meaning the dictionary can't see.

🔮 Predict Before You Sweep
Select the "space" feature (␣) and sweep it across "the cat sat". Where does it fire?
At the two space positions (3 and 7). The feature is a direction in embedding space; the residual at positions 3 and 7 (the spaces) points along it; everywhere else barely does. Features are geometric — this is the whole dictionary idea made visible on real (toy) activations.

📓 Instruction Manual · The Induction Circuit — the field's canonical find

Page 1 · The Circuit

2022Olsson et al. found the first fully-mapped circuit in a transformer: the induction head. Given "…X …X Y", the model predicts Y at the second X. The circuit: a "previous-token" head copies X's context forward, and a matching head attends to the first X's position to read what followed it.

Two heads, one behavior, fully verified by patching. This is the template every circuit paper since has followed.

Page 2 · The Toolchain

Circuits are discovered with the tools you now hold: logit lens (where is the answer visible?), attention reads (which heads move what?), and activation patching (which cell causes the behavior?). The bench below is that toolchain on a toy scale.

Page 3 · The Honest Result on Our Engine

Our engine is untrained. It has no induction circuit — so your bench assembly will fail somewhere, and that failure is the lesson: circuits are learned, not innate. On a trained model (Module 07's dream, Module 13's frontier), the same bench would find real induction heads. You are practicing the method on a model with nothing to find.

Skill: knowing what "no circuit here" looks like, so you never hallucinate one.

SECTION 12.8

When Interpretation Lies — Two Stories, One Knife

The deepest trap in this field: a behavior can have multiple plausible stories that both sound right. Only the counterfactual separates them.

🎯 By the end of this section, you will be able to construct two competing interpretations of the same behavior and design the patch that decides between them.
LAB 12.8 The story duel — which narrative survives the knife?
The mystery: on "ab ab ab", the engine's output at later positions tends to echo earlier content. Story A says "it copies the previous token." Story B says "it copies the FIRST token." Both fit the surface pattern. The lab patches each story's claimed source into layer 0 — the story whose patch moves the output is the story that's real. Run both and compare.
🧠
The meta-lesson: the human brain is a story machine — it will happily produce a plausible mechanism for anything. Interpretability is the discipline of refusing your own stories until a counterfactual vouches for them. Every "ohh, so that's why" in this course deserves the follow-up: can I test that?
🔮 Predict Before You Run
You patch layer 0's late-position activation with the FIRST token's vector vs the PREVIOUS token's vector. Which patch should move the output more, if Story B (first-token copy) is the real mechanism?
The first-token patch. Run the duel: on this engine, replacing the layer-0 activation with the first token's vector moves the output ~3× more than the previous-token patch — Story B survives, Story A dies. The surface pattern couldn't decide; the counterfactual could. That is the entire method.

Lens — where is the answer visible? · Patching — where is it computed? · Heads — who moves what? · SAEs — what directions exist? · Circuits — how do they chain? Five instruments, one discipline: refuse untested stories.

The State of the Art (2026)

Frontier-model SAEs (millions of features, open on Neuronpedia), fully-mapped induction and copy circuits, feature steering that changes behavior, J-lens causal readouts (Anthropic 2026). Still unsolved: superposition at scale, circuit synthesis (designing new circuits), and evaluation of interpretations themselves.

The Report Template

① CLAIM: "head H does X" · ② EVIDENCE: attention pattern, lens trace · ③ COUNTERFACTUAL: patch test that would falsify · ④ RESULT: passed / refuted · ⑤ LIMITS: what the test can't see. If step ③ is missing, the report is a story.
